# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People learning project management fundamentals by playing a career-sim game in the
browser, solo, in sessions of roughly 20 to 45 minutes per project. Likely students,
early-career PMs, or career-changers rather than working professionals using this at a job.
They're here to learn PM concepts (terminology, Gantt/Kanban/critical path, risk management,
stakeholder trust, SWOT and contingency planning) by making decisions and seeing consequences,
not by reading documentation.

## Product Purpose

Teach real project-management skills through an engaging simulation instead of a dry course.
The player creates a PM character, gets promoted onto a project, hires a team, plans the
work, then runs it week by week: assigning tasks, managing risk, handling events, keeping
stakeholders happy. Success looks like players finishing a project (win or lose) understanding
the PM concepts better than when they started, and wanting to run another project with a
different build.

## Brand Personality

Playful career-sim: energetic, competent, a little cheeky. It should feel like leveling up a
career, not filling out enterprise forms. Momentum and good decisions are rewarded visibly.
The tone respects that the underlying content is real and useful (this is a teaching tool),
so the personality lives in color, motion, and voice, not in gimmicks that undercut its
credibility.

## Anti-references

Not a generic SaaS or enterprise dashboard: no card-grid-of-metrics look, no corporate
blue-and-white palette, nothing that reads like an internal admin tool or a B2B analytics
product. Flat grey panels with no hierarchy and a default system sans-serif are exactly the
failure mode to avoid.

## Design Principles

- **Momentum over metrics.** Progress should feel earned and visible, not just reported in a
  grid of numbers.
- **Action within reach.** The core weekly loop (review state, decide, hit Advance Week)
  should never require scroll-hunting between the info that informs a decision and the
  control that acts on it.
- **Teach through friction, not lecture.** Mechanics like the terminology quiz, SWOT, and
  contingency planning should feel like gameplay decisions with stakes, not forms to fill out.
- **Competence-flavored fun.** Personality shows up in copy, motion, and color choices, not in
  cutesy decoration that would undercut the game's credibility as an actual teaching tool.
- **Legible under pressure.** This is a decision-dense game (schedules, budgets, risk).
  Clarity wins over decoration whenever they conflict.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA color contrast minimum. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Status is never conveyed by
color alone (pair color with a label or icon), consistent with the existing Pill component
pattern.
