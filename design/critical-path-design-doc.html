Critical Path

A career sim about actually running a software project

You play a software PM working your way up from junior hire to industry name. Each project runs like a real one: you build a risk register before anything catches fire, hire a team under a real budget, and grind through the weekly rhythm of standups, burn-down, and scope fights. The numbers on screen are the curriculum. CPI and SPI aren't decoration, they're your actual scorecard, and any risk you skip at kickoff comes back later as a crisis. This doc scopes a single deep project loop, software and IT flavored, with no accounts or backend required.

The Core Loop

One project, six beats

The full arc of a single playthrough. Everything else in this doc is detail hung on these six steps.

01

Kickoff

Brief lands with scope, budget, deadline, and a team cap. Choose a methodology, then draft the initial risk register from a candidate list. Whatever you leave off becomes a crisis with a much higher chance of hitting later.

02

Staff up

Hire from a candidate pool under budget pressure. Interview to reveal hidden traits before you commit.

03

The weekbeat

Allocate hours, watch the dashboard move, run ceremonies. The repeating heartbeat of the game.

04

Crisis events

Random rolls surface a decision, not a cutscene: a quit, a vendor delay, a scope-change request. Stats and prior planning shape your odds, not your dialogue choice alone.

05

Stakeholder review

Periodic status meetings. What you emphasize and how you frame bad news moves a trust meter that can end the project early if it collapses.

06

Delivery & debrief

Scored on time, budget, scope, morale, and trust. The debrief ties every miss back to the decision that caused it, which is where the real teaching happens.

Stat pool

  • Leadership: team morale decay rate, conflict resolution odds
  • Technical Acumen: spotting bad estimates, catching tech debt early
  • Communication: stakeholder trust swing per status update
  • Risk Management: how much of the risk register you can see and mitigate
  • Negotiation: odds of talking down scope creep or budget cuts
  • Organization: planning accuracy, ceremony effectiveness

At risk: cost and schedule slipping

SPI/CPI below 1.0 means you're behind on cost or schedule. It's recoverable, but every week you ignore it compounds. Waterfall projects surface this through a Gantt and critical-path view instead of a sprint burn-down.

Kickoff Choice

Methodology changes the mechanics, not just the label

Waterfall

  • Phase-gate reviews
  • Gantt / critical-path view
  • Formal change-control on scope

Scrum

  • Sprint planning & retros
  • Velocity & backlog
  • Standups surface small events early

Hybrid

  • Gate reviews at milestones
  • Sprint execution in between
  • Hardest to run well; highest ceiling

Scoping v0.1

Build now vs. build later

Build now

  • Character creation + stat allocation
  • One project brief, Scrum only
  • Hiring from a fixed candidate pool
  • Weekly cycle + EVM dashboard
  • ~6–8 scripted crisis events
  • 3 stakeholder trust meters
  • Scorecard + debrief screen
  • Local save (single slot)

Later

  • Career mode across many projects
  • Waterfall / Hybrid methodologies
  • Reputation & certification tree
  • Procedural brief generation
  • Team members persisting between projects
  • Rival PMs and a reputation leaderboard
  • Multiple industries
Implementation

Suggested stack

Single-player, no accounts, so no backend is needed for v0.1.

React + Vite + TS Zustand (game state) localStorage save Vitest (event/EVM logic)

Keeping game state as a single serializable store keeps the debrief screen honest: it's just reading back the same numbers the dashboard already showed.
